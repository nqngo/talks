## The Vietnamese Phonetic Structure

 Vietnamese is a **monosyllabic** language.

Most **morphemes** consists of a **single syllable\*\***.

Heavy reliance on **tones**.

Systematic **phonemic glottalisation**.

Note:

A morpheme is the smallest grammatical unit in a language. In Vietnamese, most morphemes are monosyllabic, meaning they consist of a single syllable. This is in contrast to languages like English, where morphemes can be polysyllabic (e.g., "unhappiness" consists of three morphemes: "un-", "happy", and "-ness").

\*\*: While Vietnamese is primarily monosyllabic, it does allow for compound words and phrases that can consist of multiple syllables. However, each morpheme within these compounds is still typically monosyllabic. Loanwords such as "tivi" (TV) and "cà phê" (Coffee) are also exceptions to this rule.

Vietnamese has a rich system of tones, which are variations in pitch that can change the meaning of a word. For example, "ma" (ghost), "mả" (grave), "mạ"(seedling), "má" (mother) all have different meanings. This reliance on tones is a key feature of Vietnamese phonetics and sets it apart from many other languages, including English, which does not use tones in the same way.

Contrary to English, our glottalisation is systematic. We do not flow our sounds together. Instead, we often use glottal stops to separate syllables.

---

Modern scholars such as [*Phạm (2023)*](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9780203500088/vietnamese-tone-laurence-horn-andrea-hoa-pham) condensed the syllabic scheme into the formula:

 - **(C(w)) V (G, N or T)**

*where*

 - C = Consonant
 - w = pre-nuclear glide
 - V = Vowel
 - G = Syllabic off-glide
 - N = Nasal coda
 - T = Voiceless obstruent coda

Note:

The () indicates optional elements.

## The English Phonetic Structure

English is a **polysyllabic** language.

Syllables can have **complex structures** with **clusters of consonants**.

**Non-tonal**, **Stress-timed** rhythm.

Variable **syllabic glottalisation**.

---

English syllablic scheme is commonly [cited](https://wals.info/chapter/12) as:

- **(C)(C)(C) V (C)(C)(C)**

*where*

- C = Consonant
- V = Vowel

## Implications for Leaners from Vietnam

- ❌ Drop too many consonants.
- → English words often **need clear final sounds**.
- ❌ Don’t stress vowels enough.
- → English has strong and weak syllables – **stress matters**.
- ❌ Cram too much into one syllable.
- → Break it down and slow it down. English words often **spread sounds out**

---

- ❌ Use tone instead of intonation.
- → English uses **pitch for emotion and emphasis**, not meaning.
- ❌ Speak in chunks.
- → English likes linking. Sounds should **flow together**.
- ❌ Under enunciate.
- → Try to **over enunciate**. English is a **stress-timed** language.
